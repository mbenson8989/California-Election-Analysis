**Re: Request for public guidance on whether California cast vote records are open to the public under the California Public Records Act, and for help preserving 2026 election records under the Guarantee Clause framing of Executive Order 14399**

Dear Attorney General Bonta:

## 1. Who I am and why I am writing

"The United States shall guarantee to every State in this Union a Republican Form of Government." U.S. Const. art. IV, § 4. A republican form of government rests on elections the public can check for itself. I write to respectfully ask your office to help California's county elections officials and the public do exactly that with the records California law already creates.

I am Morissa Benson, and I write regarding public access to California election records. I have studied the certified 2022 and the unofficial 2026 California governor results, county by county, using only data from the California Secretary of State. I am not alleging that any California registrar of voters, county clerk, elections official, vendor, or employee did anything improper, and I am not alleging that any California result is wrong. I am asking for two things: (1) a clear public statement of how the California Public Records Act applies to cast vote records, and (2) your office's help in making sure the records needed to answer questions about the November 3, 2026 general election are preserved and made available as the law provides.

## 2. The federal framing: Executive Order 14399

On March 31, 2026, the President signed Executive Order 14399, "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections," 91 Fed. Reg. 17125 (Apr. 3, 2026). Its authority clause rests in part on "the Federal Government's constitutional obligation to guarantee a republican form of Government to every State in the Union, U.S. Const. Art. IV, Sec. 4." Section 1 adds that "[t]he Federal Government has an unavoidable duty under Article II of the Constitution of the United States to enforce Federal law, which includes preventing violations of Federal criminal law and maintaining public confidence in election outcomes." That duty traces to the President's charge to "take Care that the Laws be faithfully executed." U.S. Const. art. II, § 3.

Section 5 of the Order states that "States and localities should preserve, for a 5-year period, all records and materials—excluding ballots cast—evidencing voter participation in any Federal election (e.g., ballot envelopes, regardless of carrier)."

I recognize the limits of that framing. The Order directs federal officers, not your office; Section 5's preservation language is phrased as "should"; Section 7(c) says the Order creates no enforceable right; and parts of the Order have been in litigation. I cite it not as a command to California but because it states, at the federal level, the same public interest the people of California wrote into their Constitution: "The people have the right of access to information concerning the conduct of the people's business, and, therefore, the meetings of public bodies and the writings of public officials and agencies shall be open to public scrutiny." Cal. Const. art. I, § 3(b)(1). The Legislature has likewise declared "that access to information concerning the conduct of the people's business is a fundamental and necessary right of every person in this state." Gov. Code § 7921.000.

## 3. What California law already provides

**Electronic records are public records.** The California Public Records Act defines "public records" to include "any writing containing information relating to the conduct of the public's business prepared, owned, used, or retained by any state or local agency regardless of physical form or characteristics." Gov. Code § 7920.530(a). An agency must, "within 10 days from receipt of the request, determine whether the request, in whole or in part, seeks copies of disclosable public records in the possession of the agency." Gov. Code § 7922.535(a). Information held electronically must be made "available in an electronic format when requested by any person." Gov. Code § 7922.570(a). "An agency shall justify withholding any record by demonstrating that the record in question is exempt under express provisions of this division, or that on the facts of the particular case the public interest served by not disclosing the record clearly outweighs the public interest served by disclosure of the record." Gov. Code § 7922.000. And "[a]ny reasonably segregable portion of a record shall be available for inspection by any person requesting the record after deletion of the portions that are exempted by law." Gov. Code § 7922.525(b). Statutes that limit access are to be "narrowly construed." Cal. Const. art. I, § 3(b)(2).

**Ballot secrecy.** "Voting shall be secret." Cal. Const. art. II, § 7. Any public access to a cast vote record therefore has to be consistent with ballot secrecy, which is why the requests below are limited to records from which voter-identifying information has been removed.

**Preservation rules.** For elections in which candidates for federal office are voted upon, the packages containing voted ballots, "[p]aper cast vote records, as defined by Sections 305.5 and 19271," vote-by-mail identification envelopes, and the other listed items "shall be kept by the elections official, unopened and unaltered, for 22 months from the date of the election." Elec. Code § 17301(b). The November 3, 2026 ballot included United States Representative, so the 22-month rule applies. Federal law separately requires that records of elections for federal office be kept for 22 months. 52 U.S.C. § 20701.

**Manual tally and voting-system rules.** During the official canvass, the elections official must conduct "a public manual tally of the ballots tabulated by those devices, including vote by mail ballots," using one of the two methods the section sets out; under the precinct method the tally covers 1 percent of the precincts chosen at random, and the elections official "may, at the elections official's discretion, select additional precincts for the manual tally." Elec. Code § 15360(a), (a)(1)(A), (a)(1)(B)(ii). The tally "shall be a public process," with at least a five-day public notice, and the report of the 1 percent manual tally "shall identify any discrepancies between the machine count and the manual tally and a description of how each of these discrepancies was resolved." Elec. Code § 15360(d), (e). A voting system must include "[a] permanent central system known to be running unaltered, certified software and firmware that is used solely to define elections and program voting equipment and memory cards." Elec. Code § 19205(d)(2)(A).

**Where California has no exact parallel.** Two points deserve candor. First, the Elections Code defines a "paper cast vote record" as "an auditable document that corresponds to the selection made on the voter's ballot and lists the contests on the ballot and the voter's selections for those contests," Elec. Code § 305.5(a), and requires that paper cast vote records be kept unopened, but I have not found a provision that expressly addresses public access to an electronic cast vote record export, either to open it or to close it. Second, I have not found a statute that, in so many words, requires a county to record or retain hash-validation results for its voting system for a set period. Whether an electronic cast vote record that contains no voter's name may be withheld, and how Gov. Code § 7922.525(b) applies to it, is where guidance from your office would help most.

## 4. Why this matters for California now

My county-level comparison of the 2022 and 2026 governor races covers all 58 California counties and was checked cell by cell, by two independent programs, against the Secretary of State's own files. California has party registration, so I compare each county's Democratic share of ballots cast with its Democratic share of registered voters. The 2026 figures are unofficial until the Secretary of State certifies the results, scheduled for December 11, 2026. What I have is an analytical model and a hypothesis, not proof. The only way to test a hypothesis like mine fairly is with the underlying records: cast vote records, ballot images where kept, and the testing and manual-tally records described above. If those records show nothing unusual, that is a result worth having too.

## 5. Specific requests

I respectfully ask your office to:

1. State publicly, in whatever form your office considers appropriate, whether electronic cast vote records from the November 3, 2026 general election are public records subject to inspection and copying under the California Public Records Act once the official canvass is complete, and whether any statute restricts disclosure of a cast vote record that contains no voter's name or other identifying information. I understand that formal opinions under Gov. Code § 12519 are given on questions submitted by the officials that section names, so I ask for public guidance rather than a formal opinion.
2. State your office's view on whether an agency whose tabulation system can export cast vote records must provide an existing export in electronic form in response to a request (Gov. Code § 7922.570), and how Gov. Code § 7922.525(b) applies if part of a record is exempt.
3. Remind county elections officials, in coordination with the Secretary of State, that ballots, paper and electronic cast vote records, ballot images, voting-system testing records, and the 1 percent manual tally records under Elec. Code § 15360 for the 2026 general election should be kept for at least the full 22-month period, and consider encouraging the five-year retention of participation records described in Section 5 of Executive Order 14399.
4. If your office finds it warranted, review whether counties are responding to requests for 2026 election records as the California Public Records Act requires, and share the results with the public.

I would be grateful for a written response within 30 days of the date of this letter. I am glad to provide my county-level data and methods to your staff.

## 6. Closing

Californians, and the officials who run their elections, deserve records that let anyone check the count. California's Constitution already declares that the writings of public agencies "shall be open to public scrutiny." A clear statement from your office would help registrars and county clerks apply the law the same way in all 58 counties, and it would help the public trust the result, whatever it shows.

Thank you for your attention to this request.

Respectfully,

Morissa Benson
